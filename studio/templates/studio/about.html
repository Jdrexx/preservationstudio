About the studio

The studio

Custom framing is expensive and the knowledge behind it has always been hard to access. We're changing that.

№ 01 — Bio

Asher Cano is a framer, artist, and producer based in Los Angeles.

With nearly a decade in the framing trade, he brings the kind of knowledge that only comes from years inside a working shop.

He is the Gallery Producer at Level Ground, a Los Angeles nonprofit arts organization, where he produces exhibitions and manages artist relationships.

His work spans public art installations, commissions for the City of Pasadena, and projects with brands and private clients across art preservation, experiential design, and object and furniture.

№ 02 — Philosophy

Custom framing is expensive and the knowledge behind it has always been hard to access.

Independent shops don't have the capacity to teach and no formal path into the trade exists for most people.

In a world moving toward digital and automated everything, knowing how to do something real with your hands with care, precision, and material knowledge has a different kind of value.

That knowledge deserves to be shared. The ability to preserve and care for objects, art, and the things that hold memory should be accessible to everyone.

№ 03 — FAQ

Frequently asked

Do I need experience?

No prior experience needed. If you can read a ruler and follow direction you're ready.

How old do I have to be?

This program is intended for adults 18 and older.

What do I bring?

One photo or print to frame for your final piece. The piece should be no larger than 12x16. Everything else — tools, materials, and glazing — are provided.

Why is it application-based?

The cohort is small and the instruction is hands-on. Applications help ensure everyone in the room is genuinely ready to commit to six weeks of real work. The process is straightforward — there are no wrong answers!

Will this help me get a job?

This program will not place you in a job directly. What it will do is give you the foundational skills and confidence to walk into almost any frame shop and be useful from day one.

Most shops don't have the bandwidth to train from scratch — arriving with real knowledge changes that conversation entirely.

Why only 6 people?

Framing requires repetition, attention to detail, and individual feedback. A smaller group means more hands-on time and instruction.

What does a typical session look like?

Each session ranges from 2–2.5 hours. The first portion is a demo and brief discussion.

The majority of the time is hands-on practice. By the end of the program sessions shift to individual work on your final piece with direct guidance.

What will I walk away with?

A finished, framed piece you built yourself.

Working knowledge of major framing techniques and an understanding of archival and conservation standards.

The ability to price and quote framing work, and alumni access to future cohorts and any group shows.

Is tuition refundable?

The $500 deposit is non-refundable once your spot is confirmed.

If something comes up before the program begins reach out directly — situations are handled case by case.

Is there a payment plan?

Payment plans are available. A $50 administrative fee applies.

Option A — total $2,550: $550 deposit (due upon acceptance, non-refundable, holds your spot) · $1,000 before Week 1 · $1,050 before Week 4 (includes $50 admin fee)

Option B — total $2,550: $500 deposit upon acceptance · $700 before Week 1 · $700 before Week 3 · $650 before Week 6 (includes $50 admin fee)

Is there an application fee?

Yes, a $25 non-refundable application fee is required to be considered. Fee waivers are available, no questions asked. Email us before submitting if you need one.

What is the sponsored seat?

Up to two sponsored seats may be available per cohort for QTPOC artists demonstrating financial need and a serious commitment to the work.

Full tuition is covered when a seat is funded. Sponsored seats are made possible through private donors, organizations, and grants.

Availability varies by cohort. If you'd like to be considered, indicate it on your application — we'll reach out if a sponsored seat is available for your cohort.

Can organizations sponsor a seat?

Yes. If your organization is interested in sponsoring a seat for a QTPOC artist, reach out directly.

Sponsoring organizations and donors receive named recognition on the program page and all relevant cohort materials, social media acknowledgment, and named recognition in the annual group show program. Sponsors are invited to the end of year group show.

For grant-funded sponsorships a formal letter of acknowledgment and outcome documentation is provided for reporting purposes.

What if I miss a session?

Because the cohort is small and the sessions build on each other, consistent attendance is expected. It matters for your own learning and for the group. However, if something comes up please reach out as early as possible.

Do I need my own tools after the program?

The program gives you the knowledge to work with tools in any shop. If you want to practice independently after the program, a basic starter kit is a relatively small investment — happy to advise on what to prioritize.

Where is it located?

Los Angeles. The exact location is shared with accepted applicants.

Do you offer the program outside of LA?

Yes — traveling weekend intensives happen periodically throughout the year. Join the mailing list to be notified when one is coming to your city.
